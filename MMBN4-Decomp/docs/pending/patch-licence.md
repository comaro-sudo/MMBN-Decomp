# Pending owner decision: licence for the patch sources and the `.bps`

Written 2026-11-27 for the owner. **Facts and consequences only; I recommend nothing.** Not legal advice. Tool licences and their sources are in [`../credits.md`](../credits.md). Open item in `docs/release-checklist.md` section 1.

## What is being licensed

- **The patch sources:** `patch/` (the armips `.asm` packages, `build.py`, `patch/tools/*.py`, our `.tpl` text scripts) and, if the owner wants the same licence, the docs.
- **The `.bps` files:** `bn4_rs_overhaul.bps` and `bn4_bm_overhaul.bps`, built with `--public`.
- **Not in scope:** `decomp/` (Gemini's, a different question: it holds Capcom-derived disassembly), and the ports, which are already GPL-3.0 (`D-EL3`).

## Facts that hold under every option

1. **No licence can cover Capcom's material.** A licence on our files gives rights only over *our* contribution. *Mega Man Battle Network 4*, its names, art and text stay Capcom's. A `.bps` is a diff against the player's ROM and is useless without it, but whether a diff is itself a derivative work is a legal question nobody here has settled. Picking CC0 or any licence does not change that.
2. **The tools' licences do not reach our files.** armips (MIT) and TextPet (MIT) only produce bytes; Flips (GPL-3.0-or-later) is a separate program, and its `COPYING` claims no rights over the files it creates ("The author claims no copyright or other rights over input, output, or error messages generated by..."). Nothing in `credits.md` forces a licence on the patch.
3. **One third-party work is inside the sources:** `wp_a1_woodman_fix.asm` re-creates Prof. 9's WoodMan fix byte-identical. His readme grants "free to use ... in any way you like. Attribution is welcome, but not necessary" and carries no licence file. That grant does not conflict with any option below, but it is a readme permission, not a formal licence, so we can license our *file* yet cannot relicense *his work*. Keep his credit in the header.
4. **`ext/mmbn-chip-tables` (StraDaMa) has no licence.** Build-time input only; no file is in the repo. This holds under all options as long as it stays outside the repo.
5. **Symbol names from Cyberworld Endless (MIT)** need its copyright notice kept if a substantial portion is copied into a licensed file. **bn6f/exe4rs have no licence:** copying names from them has no stated permission under any option.
6. **A licence is hard to withdraw.** Releases already out stay under the licence they went out with.
7. The ports are GPL-3.0. They link mGBA (MPL-2.0, compatible with GPL-3.0) and SDL3 (zlib, compatible). The ports do not include the patch sources, so the patch's licence does not bind the ports and vice versa, unless the ports start to build the patch in.

## Option A: GPL-3.0-or-later (same as the ports)

- **Means:** anyone may use, change and redistribute the sources and the `.bps`, but a distributed derivative must be under GPL-3.0 with its source. Ships with a `LICENSE` file; source files carry `SPDX-License-Identifier: GPL-3.0-or-later` (the Wii U port already does).
- **Consequences:**
  - one licence for the whole project, simple to explain;
  - copyleft: a hack that builds on our packages (for example a different overhaul reusing `wp_d3_lobby_rematches.asm`) must publish its sources under GPL-3.0;
  - compatible with the ports, with Flips (GPL-3.0+), and with MIT/zlib/MPL-2.0 inputs; incompatible with GPL-2.0-only code (the NDS port's GBARunner licence is still open, `D-EL5`; the patch uses none of it);
  - a `.bps` is a binary patch, so "source" for it means our sources plus the build instructions; the sources are public in the repo, which meets that;
  - Prof. 9's fix inside `wp_a1_woodman_fix.asm` is not affected: his permission is free.

## Option B: MIT

- **Means:** anyone may use, change and redistribute, closed or open, if they keep our copyright notice and the licence text.
- **Consequences:**
  - the most compatible permissive choice: GPL-3.0 ports and anyone else can take our code, and nothing obliges them to share changes;
  - mismatched with the ports (ports GPL-3.0, patch MIT): fine legally, but the project then has two licences to describe;
  - other ROM hackers can fold our packages into closed or paid hacks; the notice requirement is the only duty;
  - same MIT family as armips, TextPet and Cyberworld Endless, so copying a few lines from those would not change the licence.

## Option C: CC0 1.0 (public-domain dedication)

- **Means:** the owner waives, as far as the law allows, all copyright over the sources and the `.bps`; no notice or attribution is required of anyone.
- **Consequences:**
  - maximum freedom, no credit duty (credits would be voluntary, listed in the README);
  - cannot be taken back, and it covers *only* our own text and code. It must not be applied to anything not ours: the WoodMan re-creation (Prof. 9's work), and any copied MIT-licensed symbol names that need their notice kept. Those files would need to be left out or marked as exceptions;
  - CC0 is not on the OSI's approved-licence list (the FSF lists it as GPL-compatible); some contributors and companies avoid it, and a few jurisdictions treat the waiver as a licence fallback;
  - a CC0 `.bps` makes no claim about Capcom's rights, same as in fact 1.

## Variants any option can add

- **Docs and text:** `docs/` and the `.tpl` scripts could take CC BY 4.0 or follow the code licence. The `.tpl` text in the patch (Duo and Grand Prix Power names and descriptions) is our own wording.
- **Credit line:** with A or B, the licence text already carries our name; with C there is none, so a credit line is optional.
- **`decomp/`:** separate decision; it holds Capcom-derived disassembly. It is not covered here.

## What the owner needs to answer

1. Which option for the sources (A, B or C)? Same choice for the `.bps`?
2. Whose name goes in the copyright line (a person, a handle, or "the Violet Eclipse authors")? Several packages were written by agents (Gemini, Claude); who holds their copyright is not settled by anything in the repo.
3. Do the docs follow the same licence?
4. Do you want the licence recorded in a root `LICENSE` and in `patch/`, or a `LICENSE` in each of `patch/` and `ports/`? (There is none today; the Wii U sources carry SPDX headers.)
